How we quietly lost the web to sticky junk

By A. Reader · 7 min read

You came here to read one thing. Instead, a header follows you down the page, a cookie bar squats at the bottom, a modal begs for your email, and a chat bubble waves from the corner. Worst of all — the page won't even scroll.

It wasn't always like this. The modern article is a thin column of text wrapped in three layers of interruption, each one "just one tap" to dismiss, none of them staying dismissed.

What if one click could strip all of it away and simply give you the page back — and give it back exactly as it was the moment you changed your mind?

Below the fold there is more writing like this: calm, readable, uninterrupted. The kind of page the web was supposed to be before every surface became a placement.

Keep scrolling. There is nothing in your way now.

More text continues here so the scroll is long and obvious during the demo, line after line, paragraph after paragraph, proving the page moves freely once the junk is gone.

And still more, because a good demo shows real reading, not a jump cut. The column glides; the header is gone; the bars are gone; the modal is gone.

The end — or the beginning, depending on whether you kept the junk.
